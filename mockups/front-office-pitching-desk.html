Stuff & shape

FREE TABLEPRO DRILL-DOWN

Is the raw material good? Front offices grade each pitch on its physics: speed, movement, spin, where it's released and the angle it arrives. These settle within a few starts, long before ERA means anything.

Arsenal
Every pitch, graded against league average for that pitch type. Click a row to focus it in the charts.
SAVANTEDGE CALC
IVB induced vertical break: rise relative to a spinless pitch. HB horizontal break, arm side positive (mirrored for lefties so both hands compare). Active spin share of spin that creates movement (Savant). VAA vertical approach angle at the plate, computed from release velocity and acceleration; flatter four-seamers play up in the zone. RV/100 run value per 100 pitches, pitcher's view (positive = good). Stuff Grade: The Edge's own model, coming soon.
Shading: deep = 1.5+ SD from league average for the pitch type, light = 0.5–1.5 SD.
Movement profile
Each dot is a pitch. Hollow rings are league average for that type.
SAVANT
Inches of induced break from the catcher's view, mirrored so arm side is always right. The dashed line is the pitcher's arm angle. Separation between pitches that start on the same line is what makes hitters guess wrong.
Release point
Tight, overlapping release points hide which pitch is coming.
SAVANT
Release side and height in feet, catcher's view. Spread is the average distance of each pitch type's release centre from the overall centre; lower means pitches look alike out of the hand. Extension is how far in front of the rubber he lets go; more extension shortens the hitter's reaction time.
Shape comps PRO
The five league pitches closest in shape to his, and how they performed. How front offices spot a pitch that should be working better.
SAVANTEDGE CALC
Nearest neighbours on standardised velocity, IVB, HB, spin rate, extension, arm angle and VAA (z-scored within pitch type, then Euclidean distance). Similarity = 100 × e−distance. If his results trail his comps', the shape isn't the problem; look at location or sequencing.
§ 02

Execution & results

PRO

Is he getting the results his stuff deserves, and does he control it? Analysts strip luck out of ERA, then check whether he lands pitches where they work and wins counts.

ERA vs estimators
Five ways to say what his ERA should be.
MLB APISAVANTEDGE CALC
FIP uses only strikeouts, walks, hit batters and home runs. xFIP swaps his home runs for a league-average HR/FB rate. SIERA adds ground balls and the interaction of strikeouts and walks. xERA uses Statcast quality of contact. FIP, xFIP and SIERA are calculated by The Edge from public formulas and MLB Stats API counts.
Luck check
Stats that drift toward league average. Meter shows whether his sample means anything yet.
MLB APIEDGE CALC
Thresholds follow published reliability research: K% begins to stabilise around 70 batters faced, BB% around 170, ground-ball rate around 70 balls in play. BABIP and strand rate need multiple seasons, so big gaps from league average usually close.
Location by pitch
Catcher's view. Box = strike zone, outer ring = shadow and chase areas.
SAVANT
Pitches binned into a 5×5 grid scaled to each batter's strike zone. Uses the batter-side control above. Cells with fewer than 15 pitches are greyed in the live build.
Command & discipline
Bars run from league average.
SAVANT
Zone% pitches in the strike zone. Chase% swings at pitches outside it. Z-Contact% contact on swings in the zone (lower is better for the pitcher). SwStr% whiffs per pitch. First-pitch strike% strikes on 0-0.
Pitch mix by count
How he sequences: what he trusts when ahead, behind, and with two strikes.
SAVANT
Ahead = more strikes than balls; behind = more balls than strikes; two strikes includes every 2-strike count. A pitcher who throws the same mix in every count is easier to sit on.
Rolling form
Is the process trending? Markers show when the sample became meaningful.
MLB APISAVANT
Rolling average across the chosen number of appearances, weighted by batters faced. Vertical markers: cumulative 70 BF (K% begins to stabilise) and 170 BF (BB%).
