§ 05Counts, in detail

The count changes what the batter is looking for and what the pitcher can afford to throw, so the same sinker can play differently in different counts. Each box is one exact count: how often he threw the sinker there and what happened.

By exact count

Top number = whiff% per swing; below = share of his sinkers thrown in that count; n = swings. Boxes under 30 swings show — (his own sample is small, so this gate is lower than the league one).

What the count states mean

Ahead — more strikes than balls: the batter protects and chases more. Even — balanced. Behind — more balls than strikes: he has to throw a strike, the batter can sit on it.

§ 10Method

360° path. Statcast gives, for every pitch, its velocity and acceleration at 50 feet from the plate and where it crossed the plate. We anchor on the plate and run the path back to the measured release height; across 800 sampled pitches that agrees with the listed release point to 0.3 in sideways and 1 in vertically. The silhouette is illustrative.

Boxes and gates. A league box needs 150 swings before we show a whiff rate (100 batted balls for contact quality). Smaller boxes are hatched or empty — we don't estimate. Your own pitcher's splits use a 30-swing floor because the sample is a single pitcher's season, and say so.

What this is not. Everything here describes pitches that already happened. A row such as "+2 in ride" is what league pitches in that box produced, not a promise about any particular pitcher.
